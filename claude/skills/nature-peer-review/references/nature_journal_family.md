# Nature Journal Family — Differential Expectations

The Nature family is not monolithic. A reviewer report should be calibrated to the specific journal the manuscript targets. This document summarizes the dominant differences as of 2024–2025.

## Quick comparison table

| Journal | Audience | Advance bar | Article length | Main figs | Abstract | Methods placement |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Nature** | Broad science | Conceptual or empirical breakthrough; cross-discipline interest | ~5,000 words (Article) | 4 | ≤ 200 words | At end, "Online Methods" |
| **Nature Medicine** | Translational / clinical | Clinical-translational impact; rigorous human data | ~4,500 words (Article) | 5 | ≤ 250 words | At end |
| **Nature Communications** | Broad science (open access) | Significant advance, broader than specialist; less novelty pressure than Nature | ~5,000 words | 5–6 | ≤ 250 words | At end OR integrated |
| **Nature Methods** | Method developers and users across biology | Demonstrably useful new method or improvement | ~3,000 words (Article); 1,500 (Brief Comm) | 4 | ≤ 200 words | At end |
| **Nature Biotechnology** | Translation, biotech, engineering | Practical application or technology that enables new biology | ~5,000 words | 4–5 | ≤ 200 words | At end |
| **Nature Genetics** | Genetics / genomics | Significant insight from genetic data; rigorous statistical framework | ~5,000 words | 4–5 | ≤ 200 words | At end |
| **Nature Neuroscience** | Neuroscience | Significant mechanistic or systems-level insight | ~5,000 words | 4–5 | ≤ 200 words | At end |

These limits are guidelines; recent practice has loosened for high-priority papers, but the format pressure remains: **dense, compressed, broad-audience prose with rigorous Methods at the end**.

## Per-journal advance criteria

### Nature
- **Conceptual breakthrough** OR **empirical finding of broad significance**
- Should be discussable across disciplines (a neuroscientist should care about a cancer paper)
- Beware: "incremental but technically excellent" is the dominant rejection category
- Editors actively solicit reviewers' opinions on **whether the work belongs at Nature vs. Nature Communications vs. specialist journal**

### Nature Medicine
- **Clinical or translational relevance** is required, not optional
- Mouse-only papers are rare; if accepted, must have explicit human-relevance plan
- RCTs, large observational cohorts, biomarker discovery with human validation, and translational case series are core formats
- UK Biobank / All of Us / Genomics England / hospital-cohort papers are common
- **STROBE and TRIPOD compliance** strongly expected for observational and prediction-model papers
- Look for explicit framing of clinical action: *what does this paper change about patient care?*

### Nature Communications
- **Significant advance**, but the bar is "would the broad scientific community find this notable" rather than "conceptual breakthrough"
- More tolerant of specialist contributions if the science is excellent
- Open access; authors usually choose Nat Comm after transfer from Nature
- Still requires Reporting Summary, Editorial Policy Checklist, and broad-audience writing
- Reviewer reports here are often more about rigor than advance

### Nature Methods
- **Methodological advance** with demonstrated practical utility
- Must include head-to-head benchmarking against existing methods (not just citation)
- Must include code that works on independent users' data, not just authors' machines
- "Method Utility" replaces "Advance Bar": *would a competent practitioner adopt this method, and what does it enable that prior methods did not?*
- Brief Communications are a major format; do not over-demand for short papers

### Nature Biotechnology
- **Practical / translatable technology** — gene editing, protein engineering, sequencing tech, biotech platforms
- Bias toward demonstrated commercial or therapeutic potential, not just scientific interest
- Rigor in benchmarking and reproducibility is critical

### Nature Genetics
- **Genetic insight** — GWAS, rare-variant analysis, eQTL, gene-disease association
- Statistical rigor is heavily scrutinized (population stratification, multiple-testing burden, replication)
- Cohort size and diversity matter
- Replication in independent cohort often required, not optional

### Nature Neuroscience
- **Mechanistic or systems-level neuroscience insight**
- Cross-species or cross-system replication strengthens claims
- Statistical reporting (n per region, per animal, per cell) is heavily scrutinized

### Nature Reviews family (Nat Rev Cancer, Nat Rev Mol Cell Biol, etc.)
- **Critical synthesis** of a field, not original research
- Reviewer evaluates: comprehensiveness, balance, forward-looking propositions, citation quality
- Do not apply CONSORT/STROBE/TRIPOD; these are review articles

## How journal targeting changes the reviewer report

When you read a manuscript, identify the target journal from the cover letter or formatting. Then:

| Target | Emphasis |
|---|---|
| Nature | Advance Bar paragraph is the most important; do not let yourself be satisfied with "technically sound" — ask whether the advance matters |
| Nat Med | Clinical relevance and STROBE/TRIPOD rigor are the most important; reporting CIs and prediction-model performance with calibration are essential |
| Nat Comm | Rigor more than advance; reporting standards are the dominant axis |
| Nat Methods | Benchmarking, code availability, reproducibility |
| Nat Biotech | Practical utility, head-to-head technology comparison, scalability |
| Nat Genet | Statistical genetics rigor (population, multiple-testing, replication) |
| Nat Neuro | Statistical reporting per region/animal/cell; circuit-level or systems-level integration |
| Nat Reviews | Critical synthesis; citation breadth and balance |

## A common error: misaligned reviewer expectations

A frequent failure mode is reviewing a manuscript against the wrong journal's bar. Examples:

- Reviewing a Nat Comm paper at Nature's advance bar → unfairly recommending rejection
- Reviewing a Nat Med translational paper at Nature Methods' benchmarking standard → unfairly demanding head-to-head method comparisons that are not the contribution
- Reviewing a Nat Methods Brief Communication at Nature Article length → demanding more validation than the format supports

Always check the cover letter (if available) for declared target journal, and calibrate accordingly.

---

## Editorial transfer

If a paper is rejected at Nature but the science is solid, editors often offer transfer to Nature Communications or a specialist Nature journal. A reviewer who recommends "reject for journal fit, suggest transfer to Nat Comm" is doing the editor a favor — make this explicit when relevant.

Conversely, if a manuscript at Nat Comm is exceptional, a reviewer can flag this as well: "this exceeds the typical Nat Comm advance bar and might be considered for Nature."
